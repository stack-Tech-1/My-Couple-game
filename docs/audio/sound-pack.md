## Part 1. Sound effects with ElevenLabs

### Setting up (once)
1. Go to **elevenlabs.io** and sign up. The free plan gives a few credits; the Starter plan (about $5 a month) is enough for this whole list and **allows commercial use**, which we need because Amah is a paid app. Upgrade before you generate the files you'll keep.
2. In the left menu, open **Sound Effects**.

### Making each sound
1. Paste the prompt from the list into the text box.
2. Open the settings (the sliders icon):
   - **Duration:** set it to the seconds given for that sound (don't leave it on Auto).
   - **Prompt influence:** turn it up high (about 0.7), so it follows the words closely.
   - **Loop:** if you see a Loop switch, turn it **on** for every sound marked **(loop)**.
3. Press **Generate**. It gives you a few versions: play them all and keep the best one.
4. Download it and rename it to the file name in the list.

**Good to know**
- If none of the versions sound right, press Generate again. Changing one or two words also helps ("loud" or "soft", "close-up" or "far away").
- A sound with talking or music in it where there shouldn't be any: add "no voices, no music" to the end and generate again.

## Part 2. Music with Suno

### Setting up (once)
1. Go to **suno.com** and sign up.
2. **Important:** songs made on the free plan can't be used in an app you charge for. Subscribe to **Pro** before you make the songs you'll keep. Songs made while you're on Pro stay yours to use even if you cancel later.

### Making each track
1. Press **Create**, then switch on **Custom**.
2. Switch on **Instrumental** (no singing).
3. Paste the style from the list into **Style of Music**.
4. Type the file name as the **Title**.
5. Press **Create**. Suno makes two versions; listen to both and keep the one that's calmer and more even all the way through (background music shouldn't have big drops or loud endings).
6. Download it as **MP3** and rename it to the file name.

**Good to know**
- Never put an artist's name in the style: Suno refuses them, and we shouldn't copy anyone.
- About 2 minutes long is perfect; I'll make it loop smoothly.
- If a track sounds too busy, add "minimal, soft, background" to the start of the style and make it again.

## Part 3. Voices: record them yourself

Real Lagos voices beat any AI. Ask two or three friends (a man, a woman, an older person if you can) so the customers don't all sound the same.

### How to record
1. Use the phone's **voice recorder** app (Voice Memos on iPhone, Recorder on Android).
2. Find a **quiet room** with soft things in it (a bedroom with a bed and curtains is perfect; a bathroom echoes). Switch off the fan and AC.
3. Hold the phone about **a hand's length** from your mouth, a little to the side, so your breath doesn't hit the microphone.
4. Say each line **three times**, with a short pause between, and **act it**: a vexed customer should sound vexed. Bigger than you'd normally speak is right for a game.
5. **One recording per line.** Stop, then start a new one for the next line.
6. Rename each recording to the file name (if your phone won't rename, just send them in order with a note and I'll sort them).

I'll pick the best of the three takes, cut the silence and clean up the background hiss.
